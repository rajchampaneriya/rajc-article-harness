# Interview notes — issue #3

## Job of the piece

For developers building or choosing AI coding tools, an argument essay that stateless runs driven by plain git state beat long-lived remember-everything sessions — cheaper, debuggable, no drift — carried by real examples from building this harness.

## Prompts

First, one untidied take: talk or stream-type for three to five minutes without organizing it. Tell me what happened, why you want to write this now, who you want to reach, what you believe, and the examples or doubts that make the idea yours. Use real names, numbers, and incidents only when you're comfortable publishing them; skip private details instead of blurring them.

These prompts are aids, not a questionnaire to complete:

1. What happened recently that made you want to write this now — a specific conversation, bill, bug, or session that went sideways? Walk me through what actually occurred.
2. Give one real number from building this harness: a run's cost in dollars or minutes, the size of the git state you actually carry between runs, how many sessions survived before drift — whatever you actually measured, even roughly.
3. Tell me about a specific time a long-lived session's memory drifted or went stale and it bit you. What broke, and what did you do to recover?
4. Which single example from building this harness best carries the "plain git state is enough" point? What happened step by step, and what changed as a result?
5. Where are you least sure? What did going stateless cost you — re-feeding context, re-work, convenience — that a remembered session would have handled better?
6. Who disagrees with you on this, and what is the strongest version of their case you've actually heard or read? What is your honest opinion back?
7. What do you say about this out loud, to a colleague or in a chat, that you have never written down — the blunt, unhedged version?
